The renovation canvas, in four scenarios

The concept & interaction design spec, drawn. Property tree, canvas, context inspector — §3's three panes — at the two spatial fidelities §5 says must both work, and at the two moments §6 and §11 say are the hard ones: being asked for precision, and having to decide something.

This shell disagrees with the plan editor, and the disagreement is the point. That page draws SDD §60 — a layers rail, a row of drawing tools, an inspector. This one draws the concept's answer: a property tree where the layers were, one + Add where the tools were, and Work, Problem and Decision as things that live on the canvas rather than in a side panel. Both are proposals. Neither is built. Caveats in README.

Working: level 2 fidelity, Kitchen selected

§3 assembled. The plan is approximate — rooms have positions and rough adjacency, and no measurement anywhere, which is the fidelity §5 says must already carry work, cost, problems and decisions. So there is no grid: a grid is a measurement, and this plan has none. Drawn at a pane width rather than a page width — drag the right edge and watch what the rails cost.

Three numbers that had to agree. The rejected options stay on screen with their prices, so the €2,560 the guidance quotes is the spread a reader can compute (€2,900 − €340). The Bathroom's estimate does not include the €1,250, because the decision is open and §10 says the impact is unknown — the alternative would be a total that moves every time somebody clicks a radio button they have not committed to.

And one the drawing refused. This frame was built to argue that a decision belongs over the plan rather than in a modal, and drawing it argued the opposite. At the pane width above the canvas is about 456px and the card is 248 of them: docked anywhere it covers more than half the plan, and the first placement — bottom right, written down as "the emptiest corner" — landed on top of the Hallway and clipped the very pin the card belongs to. It now sits top right, over the one room in this scenario that carries nothing, and the underlying question is open rather than quietly half-solved: a decision may simply be an inspector surface, and a canvas that has to dodge its own panel is evidence for that.

Precision on demand, and what Add offers where

§6's argument is that a product must never ask for precision without saying what the precision buys. Two frames — the ask, and what answering it produced — and then §14 and §15's menus, which are the other half of the same claim: the vocabulary is small, renovation words come before CAD words, and the list gets shorter as the selection gets narrower.

The arithmetic, so it can be checked rather than believed. The polygon is 230 × 200 units less a 75 × 75 notch at 20 mm per unit — 40,375 units², 16.15 m². Waste at 10% gives 17.77 m². The three material rates apply to that 17.77; labour's €104.50 applies to the 16.15, on the laid area rather than the ordered one, which is the note under them. They sum to €2,576.18, and the notch is 1.50 m on both sides, which is the third dimension on the plan. The rough estimate this replaced was €2,500 — measuring moved the number, which is the only honest reason to have asked for the measurement.

What this page does not settle

Written down rather than left to be discovered, because a drawing that reads as settled is worse than one that admits what it skipped.

  • Which shell wins. This page and the plan editor propose two different left rails, two different toolbars and two different answers to what a canvas object is. The SDD is the authority and §60 is what it currently says, so this page is the proposal and that one is the record. Nothing here changes the SDD.
  • Nothing collapses a rail. The container ladder concept.css documents narrows the inspector and then the tree, and below roughly 620px the three panes still crowd each other. Adding a third pane's worth of content did not make that easier, and this page does not answer it either.
  • Where a decision lives. Scenario 3 docks it over the canvas and the measurement above says that costs more than half the plan at pane width. The alternative — a decision is what the inspector shows when a decision is selected — is not drawn here, so this page raises the question and answers it badly on purpose rather than answering it silently.
  • The pins scale with the plan. They are svg, in plan coordinates, so a deep zoom grows the labels. canvas.css explains why the HTML alternative was worse — it drifts from the drawing at every pane width — but "the other option was worse" is not the same as "this one is right".
  • The tree is one level deeper in scenario 3 than §3 draws it. §3's tree is spaces only; §17 requires the tree to synchronise with the selection, and a problem is not a space. Showing objects under the selected space is one reading of that, and there are others — a separate object list, or a "contains the selection" state on the room row.
  • No German. The plan editor's German scenario is what produced the rail-width finding, and every label here is English. Renovierungsbereich, Entscheidung erforderlich and Materialbedarf have not been near this layout, and the + Add menu's four group headings are the obvious place for it to break.
  • Three contrast failures survive, and none of them is this concept's. Run through axe-core in both schemes, the page reports exactly three distinct failing pairs, all of them host tokens on components §60's sheet or the page furniture already owned: --interactive-accent as a link colour and as .rp-empty .rp-action's label (3.42:1 light, 4.00:1 dark), and --text-faint on a hidden layer's name (2.12:1 light, 2.56:1 dark) — the figure the README already records. A fourth was this concept's, on + Add's accent label, and it is fixed: the label takes --text-normal and the accent rides the border and the icon. What axe cannot see here is the same list it cannot see anywhere in jsdom or a headless shot — focus-indicator visibility and hit-target size are verified in a live vault and nowhere else.
  • The frames are taller than a real pane. Two of them are 740px so their inspectors fit, and a real Obsidian pane is routinely 500–600px tall. At that height the context inspector scrolls — a heading, a meter, a count grid, fields, guidance and an action set is a lot for one column, and this page shows it with room it will not always have.
  • Not a check, with one exception this page discovered by tripping over it. Nothing in npm run check reads what is in these files — no linter, no test, no renderer. But npm run analyze walks the whole repository for unreachable files, so a stylesheet .fallowrc.json does not declare is a dead file: canvas.css arrived undeclared and took all four CI legs red on a commit that touched nothing but docs/. Contrast, focus visibility and hit-target size are still verified in a live vault (npm run test-build) and nowhere else.
