# Jobs-to-be-Done Research Backlog — Renovation Planner

**Status:** Draft / research hypotheses  
**Purpose:** Candidate JTBDs to split into individual Markdown notes and validate through interviews.

## How to use

Each job should later become a note named `JTBD-###-slug.md` with: job statement, trigger, desired progress, functional/emotional/social outcomes, current alternatives, pain points, forces of progress, interview questions, evidence, opportunity assessment, related capabilities, and validation status.

> When [situation], I want to [motivation/action], so I can [desired progress].

## Web-research grounding

The backlog intentionally covers much more than visual room planning. Recent renovation research shows why:

- Planning itself is substantial: Houzz's 2024 U.S. study reported planning taking about twice as long as construction across rooms; kitchens averaged 9.6 months planning and 5.1 months building.
- Budget control is a persistent job: the 2026 U.S. Houzz study reports 75% set an initial budget and 37% exceeded it; the 2026 UK study reports 74% budgeted and 38% exceeded it.
- Scope expansion, unexpectedly costly products/services and upgrading material choices contribute to overruns.
- Coordination with professionals is normal: 91% of U.S. and 92% of UK renovators in the 2026 Houzz studies hired professionals.
- Repair/replacement matters alongside redesign: Harvard JCHS reports need-to-do replacements accounted for 49% of aggregate homeowner improvement spending in 2023.
- Older properties increase maintenance/replacement needs, while DIY and professional work coexist.
- Consumer Reports research highlights change during construction, structural discoveries, permits and material problems as important schedule risks.

# Theme — Starting & framing

## JTBD-001 — Capture the renovation intent

> When something in my property needs to change, I want to capture what I am trying to achieve, so I can turn a vague idea into a project I can work on.

## JTBD-002 — Establish project scope

> When I begin planning, I want to define what is and is not part of the renovation, so I can understand its size and control scope growth.

## JTBD-003 — Define success

> When I start a renovation, I want to clarify what a successful result means, so I can make consistent trade-offs between cost, time, quality and appearance.

## JTBD-004 — Decide where to start

> When the renovation feels large or uncertain, I want to identify a sensible first step, so I can make progress without understanding everything upfront.

## JTBD-005 — Break the renovation into manageable parts

> When many areas and work types are involved, I want to break the project into understandable parts, so I can focus without losing the whole-project view.


# Theme — Existing property

## JTBD-006 — Capture the existing state

> When I plan changes, I want to record what exists today, so decisions are based on reality rather than memory.

## JTBD-007 — Structure the property spatially

> When I organize information, I want to structure buildings, floors, rooms and outdoor areas, so work stays connected to the place it affects.

## JTBD-008 — Capture measurements

> When dimensions affect design, quantity or cost, I want reliable measurements, so I can plan with confidence.

## JTBD-009 — Use an existing plan

> When I already have a plan, sketch or image, I want to use it as context, so I do not recreate information I possess.

## JTBD-010 — Plan without a floor plan

> When I do not have or need a usable plan, I want to organize the renovation anyway, so visualization is not a prerequisite.

## JTBD-011 — Record defects and constraints

> When I discover defects or technical constraints, I want to record them in context, so later plans account for them.


# Theme — Design & decisions

## JTBD-012 — Describe what should change

> When I focus on an area, I want to describe the desired changes, so the future state becomes concrete.

## JTBD-013 — Compare alternative solutions

> When multiple solutions are possible, I want to compare them, so I can balance cost, effort, appearance and long-term value.

## JTBD-014 — Visualize the desired result

> When the outcome is hard to imagine, I want to visualize proposed changes, so I can decide before committing resources.

## JTBD-015 — Capture inspiration in context

> When I find useful inspiration, I want to attach it to the relevant area, so it is available when decisions are made.

## JTBD-016 — Record decisions and rationale

> When I make a choice, I want to record what I decided and why, so I do not repeatedly revisit settled questions.


# Theme — Work planning

## JTBD-017 — Turn desired changes into work

> When I know what should change, I want to identify the work required, so the design becomes executable.

## JTBD-018 — See all work for a room

> When I work on a room, I want all relevant renovation work together, so I understand the complete impact on that space.

## JTBD-019 — Organize work by trade

> When specialists are needed, I want to group work by trade, so I can coordinate each professional.

## JTBD-020 — Break work into tasks

> When work is too large to act on, I want to break it into tasks, so I know what can be done next.

## JTBD-021 — Understand dependencies

> When activities depend on each other, I want to see those relationships, so I avoid wrong sequencing and rework.

## JTBD-022 — Separate must-do from nice-to-have

> When time or money is constrained, I want to distinguish essential work from optional improvements, so core outcomes are protected.


# Theme — Budget & estimation

## JTBD-023 — Set an initial budget

> When considering a renovation, I want to establish what I can spend, so I can judge realistic scope.

## JTBD-024 — Create a rough estimate

> When details are incomplete, I want to attach rough costs to work, so I understand financial scale quickly.

## JTBD-025 — Refine estimates progressively

> When knowledge improves, I want to refine earlier estimates, so the budget becomes more reliable without starting over.

## JTBD-026 — Calculate quantities

> When work depends on area, length or count, I want to derive quantities, so estimates and purchases are grounded.

## JTBD-027 — Allow for waste and contingency

> When estimating, I want to account for waste and uncertainty, so normal variation does not immediately break the budget.

## JTBD-028 — Understand cost drivers

> When many expenses accumulate, I want cost broken down by space, work, trade and material, so I know what drives the total.

## JTBD-029 — Compare estimated, committed and actual cost

> When execution begins, I want to compare estimates, orders and actual spend, so I know whether finances remain controlled.

## JTBD-030 — Evaluate scope-change impact

> When considering a change, I want to see cost and schedule consequences first, so budget creep becomes an explicit decision.


# Theme — DIY & professionals

## JTBD-031 — Decide what to DIY

> When planning work, I want to decide what I can reasonably do myself, so I can balance savings, time, quality and risk.

## JTBD-032 — Identify work needing professionals

> When work exceeds my skills, permissions or safety tolerance, I want to identify professional needs early, so they can be planned.

## JTBD-033 — Prepare contractor scope

> When requesting a quotation, I want to communicate scope, quantities, context and constraints clearly, so offers are useful and comparable.

## JTBD-034 — Compare quotations

> When I receive multiple offers, I want to compare price, scope, assumptions and exclusions, so I can choose intelligently.

## JTBD-035 — Assign responsibility

> When DIY and several trades overlap, I want to know who owns each activity, so work does not fall between responsibilities.

## JTBD-036 — Coordinate trade readiness

> When a professional is due to work, I want prerequisites, access and decisions ready, so their visit is productive.


# Theme — Materials & procurement

## JTBD-037 — Select materials and products

> When choosing what to install, I want to compare options against function, appearance, cost and maintenance, so I can select confidently.

## JTBD-038 — Connect products to work

> When I choose a product, I want to know where and why it is used, so purchasing stays tied to scope.

## JTBD-039 — Know what to buy

> When execution approaches, I want a consolidated purchase need, so I do not rebuild shopping lists from separate rooms.

## JTBD-040 — Know when to buy

> When products have lead times or storage constraints, I want to know when they are needed, so procurement supports the schedule.

## JTBD-041 — Track orders and deliveries

> When purchasing begins, I want to distinguish planned, ordered, delivered, missing and returned items, so material uncertainty does not block work.

## JTBD-042 — Preserve product information

> When something is installed, I want its model, supplier, manual, warranty and receipt preserved, so future maintenance is easier.


# Theme — Schedule & execution

## JTBD-043 — Understand overall sequence

> When many activities interact, I want to understand their broad order, so I avoid conflicts and rework.

## JTBD-044 — Plan phases

> When the project cannot happen at once, I want to group work into phases, so money, people, spaces and disruption can be managed.

## JTBD-045 — Estimate duration

> When building a schedule, I want realistic activity durations, so expectations and dependencies are credible.

## JTBD-046 — Coordinate trades and DIY

> When multiple people contribute, I want their work sequenced feasibly, so nobody arrives before prerequisites are ready.

## JTBD-047 — Identify parallel work

> When I want to shorten duration, I want to know what can safely happen in parallel, so time is saved without creating conflicts.

## JTBD-048 — Know what needs attention now

> When execution is underway, I want to see current work, decisions, purchases and blockers, so I can focus on the next useful actions.

## JTBD-049 — Track progress

> When work is underway, I want to record planned, active, blocked and completed work, so project status is reliable.

## JTBD-050 — Manage blockers

> When work cannot continue, I want to know why and what is affected, so I can resolve the constraint before delays spread.

## JTBD-051 — Minimize household disruption

> When I live in the property during renovation, I want to understand which rooms and services will be unavailable, so daily life can be planned.


# Theme — Change, documentation & completion

## JTBD-052 — Capture unexpected work

> When demolition or inspection reveals extra work, I want to add it without losing the original plan, so the project reflects reality.

## JTBD-053 — Replan after change

> When scope, price, delivery or availability changes, I want to understand downstream effects, so the plan remains coherent.

## JTBD-054 — Preserve the baseline

> When the project changes, I want the original plan retained, so I can understand how and why it evolved.

## JTBD-055 — Keep project information together

> When notes, photos, quotes and receipts accumulate, I want them organized around the renovation, so I can find what I need.

## JTBD-056 — Attach evidence to context

> When I capture a photo or document, I want it linked to the relevant room, work or product, so its meaning remains clear.

## JTBD-057 — Record renovation history

> When work changes the property, I want a history of what was done and installed, so future decisions have reliable context.

## JTBD-058 — Know when the project is finished

> When major construction ends, I want to identify remaining defects, work and documents, so the renovation does not stay permanently almost done.

## JTBD-059 — Understand final cost

> When the renovation ends, I want to know what I actually spent and where, so I can assess the financial outcome.

## JTBD-060 — Compare plan with reality

> When the project ends, I want to compare planned scope, cost and schedule with the result, so I can learn from the project.

## JTBD-061 — Preserve the as-built state

> When renovation changes the property, I want the record to reflect what actually exists, so it remains useful after completion.

## JTBD-062 — Reuse lessons

> When I start future work, I want to reuse useful costs, suppliers, products, work patterns and lessons, so I do not start from zero.

## JTBD-063 — Plan future maintenance

> When installed components require care, I want maintenance knowledge preserved, so renovation information remains useful throughout ownership.

# Suggested Interview Prompts

Avoid asking users whether they want proposed features. Ask about real past behavior:

1. Tell me about the last renovation project you started.
2. What happened between first deciding to renovate and the first physical work?
3. What did you write down or collect?
4. How did you decide what was in scope?
5. What was hardest to keep track of?
6. How did you estimate the budget?
7. When did your estimate first become wrong?
8. Tell me about something unexpected that changed the plan.
9. How did you coordinate DIY work and professionals?
10. How did you know what needed to happen next?
11. Where did plans, photos, quotes, receipts and decisions live?
12. Tell me about a time you could not find information you needed.
13. What caused delays?
14. What caused extra cost?
15. How did you decide between alternative products or solutions?
16. What did you wish you had documented earlier?
17. How did you know the project was finished?
18. What renovation information do you still use today?

For each important event, probe:

```text
What happened?
What did you do next?
Why?
What were you worried about?
What alternatives did you consider?
What information did you need?
Where did you get it?
What was frustrating?
What did that cost you in time, money or confidence?
```

# Research Evidence Standard

A job should move from `hypothesis` to `validated` only when repeated evidence shows:

- a recognizable triggering situation,
- meaningful desired progress,
- existing effort or workaround,
- consequences when the job is poorly served,
- recurrence across multiple participants or a clearly valuable segment.

Suggested status:

```text
hypothesis
→ interviewing
→ evidence-found
→ validated
→ opportunity-assessed
→ product-linked
→ rejected / niche
```

# Important Product Research Principle

Do not force interview evidence into the current Renovation Planner architecture.

If research shows that users think primarily in phases, decisions, defects, contractors, purchases, or another model rather than the proposed Project → Space → Work structure, treat that as evidence to change the product model rather than evidence to rename the user's behavior.
